Brow guide

Brow lamination
or microblading?

One is a styling treatment that wears off. The other is a tattoo. They get shelved together because both promise fuller brows, but the commitment could not be more different — and that difference should drive your decision.

The fundamental difference

Brow lamination is a chemical treatment that relaxes the hair so it can be reset in a new direction — usually brushed up and out. It uses the brow hair you already have, makes it sit fuller, and wears off gradually over six to eight weeks. Nothing is implanted and nothing is permanent.

Microblading is cosmetic tattooing. Pigment is deposited into the skin in fine strokes to create the appearance of hair where there may be none. It lasts twelve to eighteen months, requires a healing period, and needs periodic touch-ups.

Put simply: lamination restyles what grows there. Microblading draws in what does not.

Which one suits you

Lamination works if you have the hair but it misbehaves — brows that grow downward, a stubborn patch that will not lie flat, or hair long enough to cover gaps if it were pointed the right way. This covers most people who think their brows are "thin"; very often the hair is there and simply pointing the wrong way.

Microblading is for genuine absence — brows lost to over-plucking, alopecia, chemotherapy or genetics, where there is not enough hair to restyle. If there is nothing to lift, lamination cannot manufacture it.

If you are unsure, start with lamination. It is cheaper, it is reversible by simply waiting, and it tells you a great deal about what your brows can look like before you commit to a tattoo.

Risk, honestly

This is where they genuinely diverge. A poor lamination is temporary — over-processed brows feel dry or frizzy and recover within weeks. Unpleasant, not lasting.

A poor microblade is on your face for a year or more. Wrong shape, wrong colour, or pigment that heals to an unexpected tone are all common, and correction means laser or saline removal, which is slow, costs considerably more than the original work, and does not always fully succeed.

That asymmetry is the main argument for trying lamination first. This studio offers lamination, tinting, henna and shaping, but not microblading — if a tattoo is genuinely the right answer for you, go to a specialist who does it every day and ask to see healed results, not fresh ones.

Questions

Commonly asked

Is brow lamination the same as microblading?

No. Lamination is a temporary chemical treatment that resets the direction your existing brow hair grows, lasting six to eight weeks. Microblading is cosmetic tattooing that implants pigment into the skin and lasts twelve to eighteen months.

How long does brow lamination last?

Six to eight weeks, which follows the natural brow growth cycle. It fades gradually rather than stopping abruptly.

Does brow lamination damage your brows?

Not when done correctly and spaced properly. The risk is over-processing from treatments done too close together, which leaves hair dry or frizzy. Leaving at least six weeks between appointments avoids it.

Which is cheaper, brow lamination or microblading?

Lamination, by a wide margin per appointment - $70, or $85 with a tint, against several hundred dollars for microblading. Over several years the totals move closer together, but lamination carries far less risk.

Should I try lamination before microblading?

It is a sensible order. Lamination is cheaper, wears off on its own, and shows you what your brows can look like styled before you commit to something that lasts more than a year.
