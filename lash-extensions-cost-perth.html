What lash extensions
actually cost

Most price guides quote you a full set and stop there, which is how people end up surprised three weeks later. Here is the whole picture: what a set costs, what keeping it costs, and what a price that looks too good usually signals.

The honest numbers

Across Perth in 2026, a classic full set from a qualified technician sits roughly between $90 and $180, and volume between $130 and $250. The spread is mostly experience, product and how long the technician allows per client — not suburb.

The cost nobody quotes you

A full set is the entry fee, not the running cost. Extensions shed with your natural lash cycle, so a set needs a refill every two to three weeks to stay looking deliberate. That is the number that actually matters to your budget.

Realistically, budget $120 to $150 a month to keep a set going — a full set up front, then two refills a month. Over a year that is roughly $1,500 to $1,800. Anyone who tells you otherwise is quoting you the first appointment and hoping you do not do the arithmetic.

If that number does not suit, a lash lift at $75 every six to eight weeks costs around $550 a year for a fraction of the upkeep. That is a perfectly good answer and nobody should talk you out of it.

What makes one set cost more than another

Four things, in order of how much they matter:

Time allowed. A proper classic set takes about two hours and volume closer to two and a half. A studio charging $70 cannot afford to spend two hours on you, so it will not — that is arithmetic, not cynicism.

Isolation. Every natural lash must be separated before an extension is attached. Skipping it is the single fastest way to apply a set, and it is why cheap sets shed in clumps and damage the natural lash underneath.

Product. Adhesive is the main variable. Cheap adhesive is harsher, cures unpredictably and retains badly.

Experience. Mapping a set to your eye shape is judgement, and judgement is the thing that separates a set that suits you from a set that merely stayed on.

When a cheap set is a warning

A $60 full set is not a bargain, it is a signal. At that price the appointment has to be under an hour to be viable, which almost always means lashes glued together rather than isolated.

The cost of that shows up later: natural lashes pulled out as the stuck extensions shed, gaps that take a full growth cycle to recover, and sometimes months of wearing nothing. Repairing that costs more than the set you saved on.

Ask before you book: how long is the appointment, is the technician certified, and will you get a patch test. A studio confident in its work answers all three without hesitating.

Questions

Commonly asked

How much are lash extensions in Perth?

In 2026, expect $90 to $180 for a classic full set and $130 to $250 for volume from a qualified technician. At this studio a classic full set is $120, hybrid $140, volume $160 and mega volume $180.

How much do lash refills cost?

A two-week refill is $60 and a three-week refill is $75. Left past about four weeks, too little of the original set remains and it is usually priced as a new full set.

How much should I budget per month for lash extensions?

Around $120 to $150 a month once you are maintaining a set, which is roughly two refills. The first month costs more because it includes the full set.

Are cheap lash extensions worth it?

Usually not. Below about $80 a full set the appointment has to be rushed to be profitable, and the corner most often cut is isolation - which is what causes damage to your natural lashes. Repairing that costs far more than the difference.

Is a lash lift cheaper than extensions?

Significantly. A lash lift is $75 and lasts six to eight weeks with no refills, so roughly $550 a year against $1,500 or more for maintained extensions.
