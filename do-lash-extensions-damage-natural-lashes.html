Lash health guide

Do extensions damage
your natural lashes?

Extensions themselves do not damage lashes. Bad application does, and so does bad removal. The difference between a set you can wear for years and a set that leaves gaps comes down to three things you can check for yourself.

The short answer

A properly applied extension is attached to one natural lash, about a millimetre from the skin, and weighs no more than that lash can carry. It grows out with the lash and falls when the lash was going to fall anyway. Nothing about that harms the lash or the follicle.

Damage happens when one of those conditions is broken. In practice it is nearly always one of the following.

What actually causes damage

Lashes glued together. Every natural lash grows at its own speed. If two or three are stuck to one extension, the faster one drags the others out as it grows. Separating each lash before an extension goes on is called isolation, it is the slowest part of the job, and it is the first thing skipped when an appointment is rushed.

Too much weight. An extension that is too long or too thick for the natural lash bends it, and over weeks the lash sheds early and grows back finer. This is why the set you want and the set your lashes can carry are not always the same thing.

Pulling them off. Picking at extensions or peeling them off takes the natural lash with them. Proper removal dissolves the adhesive so the extension slides off, and it takes a few minutes.

Rubbing. Rubbing your eyes or dragging a towel across them does more harm with extensions on than without.

Do-it-yourself clusters. Strip-style clusters glued along the lash line at home attach to many lashes at once, which is the same problem as poor isolation.

What a damaged lash line looks like

Gaps where there used to be lashes. Lashes that are visibly shorter or finer than they were. Extensions coming away in clumps of several lashes rather than one at a time.

If that is where you are, the fix is to have the set removed properly and let your lashes grow. A lash takes six to eight weeks to go through its cycle, so expect improvement over one to two months rather than days. They do grow back.

Do you need a break from extensions?

Not on a timetable. The idea that lashes need to “breathe” is a myth. A lash is not alive past the follicle, so it has nothing to breathe with.

What matters is whether your natural lashes are healthy now. If they are, and the set is the right weight, there is no reason to stop. If they are thinning, a lighter set or a few weeks off is the answer, and at this studio you will be told that directly, even though it means a smaller booking.

What to ask before you book anywhere

How long is the appointment? A full set done properly takes around two hours. Under an hour means isolation is being skipped.

Will you tell me if my lashes cannot take the set I am asking for? The right answer is yes.

How do you remove them? The right answer involves a remover, never tweezers alone.

Questions

Commonly asked

Do lash extensions ruin your natural lashes?

No, not when they are applied properly: one extension to one natural lash, at a weight that lash can carry. Damage comes from lashes glued together, extensions that are too heavy, or pulling them off.

Will my lashes grow back after extensions?

Yes. Lashes that were pulled out or shed early regrow from the follicle. A full cycle takes six to eight weeks, so allow one to two months to see them back to normal.

Do my lashes need a break from extensions?

Not on a schedule. If your natural lashes are healthy and the set is the right weight, you can wear extensions continuously. Take a break only if your lashes are thinning.

Can I remove lash extensions myself?

It is not a good idea. Pulling or picking takes natural lashes with the extensions, and household oils only loosen them partly. A professional removal dissolves the adhesive and takes a few minutes. At the studio it is $25 as an add-on to a new set or refill.

Why do my eyes feel sore or heavy with extensions?

They should not. Soreness usually means lashes are stuck together and pulling, or an extension is touching the skin. Heaviness means the set is too heavy. Both are worth getting checked rather than waiting out.

Are volume lashes worse for your lashes than classic?

Not by themselves. A volume fan is made of much finer extensions, so a well-made fan can weigh less than a single thick classic extension. What matters is the total weight on each natural lash.
